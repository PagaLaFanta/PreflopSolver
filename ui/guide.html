PreflopSolver — User guide

A practical guide to using the solver day to day: how to set up a spot, which settings to choose, how to read the results and what to do when something looks wrong. The general overview and build instructions are in the README; the full feature list is in FEATURES.md.

The interface is in English by default. The ES button in the top bar switches it to Spanish (and EN back to English).

1. What it is and what it is not

It is a preflop tournament solver: it computes the equilibrium (approximate Nash) of a hand with specific stacks, blinds, antes and payout structure, in chip EV, ICM (including multi-table) and PKO. Play after the flop uses an abstraction (MCCFR with geometric bet sizes and hands grouped into buckets), so that the preflop strategy knows how much seeing a flop is really worth with each hand.

It is not a postflop solver. The postflop game is there to value preflop decisions correctly; its frequencies are not postflop study material. To study a specific flop, export the ranges that reach it (--export-pio) and load them into a postflop solver.

Quality. The prize models (ICM, multi-table ICM, PKO) are exact, and the strategy is validated with NashConv (how much a perfect opponent could exploit it) and with exact checks on small spots. In typical push/fold and ICM spots the solution is practically exact; in deep spots with postflop play, the frequencies of indifferent actions may vary between runs without costing any EV (see §5.4).

2. Installation and first run

Requirements

  • 64-bit Windows 10/11. Nothing to install.
  • RAM: 8 GB is enough for almost everything; large trees (deep 6-max with 3-4 way pots and 1024 buckets) can need several GB. The tree preview shows the estimate before solving.
  • CPU: all cores are used. If the processor supports AVX2, the interface automatically picks solve-avx2.exe, which is faster.

Folder contents

PreflopSolver.exeOpens the interface in its own window (Chrome or Edge in app mode).

solve.exe, solve-avx2.exeThe engine. Can also be used directly from a console.

.cache/Created automatically: equity matrix, postflop pool, FGS tables. Do not delete.

First run

Double-click PreflopSolver.exe. The first time, the engine computes and stores in .cache/:

  • the exact equity matrix (~40 s),
  • the board bunching matrices (1-3 min, with 3 or more players),
  • the postflop pool for each bucket configuration (seconds),
  • the FGS tables and ranking, if you use FGS (~1 min),
  • with multi-table ICM and many players, the field table (up to ~13 min for an MTT with 200+ players; only the first time for that structure).

After that everything comes from the cache. If a first calculation seems "stuck" in the preparation phase, this is almost always why: check the Log tab.

When you close the window, the program exits on its own within a minute. If a calculation is running, it waits until it finishes.

3. Workflow in the interface

The normal cycle is: ＋ New hand → wizard → review the tree → ▶ Solve → read the results.

3.1 Creating the hand: the wizard

＋ New hand opens the three-step wizard. It is the only place where a hand is configured.

Step 1 · Game and equity

  • Template: fills the wizard with a typical spot (MTT final table, SNG, push/fold SNG endgame, PKO final table, heads-up, chip EV 6-max). Then change the stacks and prizes to those of your hand. It is the fastest way to start.
  • 📋 Import hand history: paste or open a PokerStars, GGPoker or Winamax hand history, pick the hand, and players, stacks in bb, SB, ante (including big blind ante) and bounties are filled in. Your seat is marked with ★ and your cards.
  • Players and stacks, in bb and in action order (UTG … BTN, SB, BB). If you prefer to type chips as shown by the tournament clock, fill in BB in chips and type the stacks in chips: they are converted to bb when you accept.
  • Equity model: Chip EV, ICM or multi-table ICM. With ICM, add the remaining prizes (+ Place). With multi-table ICM, the stacks of the other tables (individually or as NxS, e.g. 16x25,2x40), or 🎲 Generate stacks from the tournament average.
  • Bounty: Off, PKO or SKO, with each player's bounty (and the average of other tables). With Chip EV, bounties are valued in chips (useful to isolate the bounty effect without ICM pressure).
  • FGS: no, 1, 2, 3 or auto. See §4.4.

Step 2 · Preflop betting

  • Profile: Push/fold (all-in or fold) or Standard (opens, 3-bets, 4-bets…).
  • Open sizes by position, BB raise over limp, SB opens.
  • By level: 3-bet/4-bet/5-bet sizes in and out of position. Grammar: 2.5bb, 3x (of the last raise), 75% (of the pot after calling), ai, 2.5bb+1bb (+1 bb per limper/caller).
  • All-in threshold: a raise that commits more than this % of the effective stack becomes an all-in.
  • ⚙ Advanced: flats per level (how many players may call without raising), closing flats (whoever closes the action can always call) and cold calls. The defaults are good; only change them to reproduce a specific tree.

Step 3 · Postflop and calculation

  • Postflop: Full (the normal choice) or Check-down (nobody bets after the flop: faster, less accurate).
  • Postflop precision: Normal (256 hand groups per street) or High (1024, slower). See §4.3.
  • ⚙ Advanced: geometric size, maximum bets, postflop raises and raises in 3-way pots. Only to reproduce a specific tree.
  • Max active players: how many players can reach a flop or an all-in together (2-6).
  • Quality: Quick (CI 20), Study (CI 10, recommended) or Reference (CI 3). There is no iteration count to choose: the calculation runs until the CI drops below the target (see §4.5).
  • 🌳 Preview tree: builds the tree without solving (instant), with action nodes, flop leaves, postflop nodes, size and estimated RAM.

Accept saves the hand and shows its tree in the Strategy tab, not solved yet.

3.2 Reviewing and pruning the tree before solving

Before pressing Solve, walk through the tree. Each ✕ next to an action prunes it from that specific node, seeing the tree before spending any calculation time. Use it to:

  • remove sizes you do not want for a position (e.g. a single open size for the SB),
  • cut absurd lines that bloat the tree.

The last action of a node cannot be pruned. Prunes are saved with the hand.

Good practice: a smaller tree converges faster and better. Every extra size multiplies the number of nodes and dilutes the iterations. Start with few sizes and only add those that change a real decision.

3.3 Solving, stopping and continuing

  • ▶ Solve starts the calculation. The top bar shows the CI live, colored by threshold (red ≥ 10, amber < 10, green ≤ 3), and an estimate of the time left to reach the chosen quality.
  • ■ Stop stops the calculation within a second and keeps what was computed.
  • ⏩ Continue loads the previous solution and keeps iterating on the same tree: up to the chosen quality if you stopped early; if it had already got there, down to half of its CI.
  • Save spot saves the configuration in spots/ for reuse.
  • Duplicate (hands panel ☰) copies the current hand without its solution: the quick way to study variants (another stack, another prize). Double-click a hand to rename it.
  • download (top bar, when finished): ranges as TSV and the solution as JSON.

3.4 Reading the solution (Strategy tab)

  • Tree: folds are flattened, so the root directly shows every position's open. Click a row to see that player's range at that node; ▸ or double-click shows the responses. With the tree focused: ← parent node, → enter, ↑↓ sibling action.
  • 13×13 grid: color by action and frequency per hand. Each cell's height is the share of that hand reaching the node (empty: it was already folded). Modes:
    • Strategy: frequencies.
    • EV: EV of each action relative to folding, in $ with ICM or in bb (unit selector).
    • EV vs best other: how much the chosen action gains over the best alternative. This is the view that actually tells you whether a decision matters.
  • Node panel: per action, frequency, combos and average EV of the combos that play it. You can also lock an action's range or frequency from here.
  • ✎ Paint lock: paint actions onto the grid and lock the node to that strategy (node locking). Locks apply when you solve again; see §6.4.
  • 🎯 Refine this spot (node panel, with a saved solution): keeps iterating only that node's subtree; see §6.5.
  • Image: downloads the node's grid as a PNG.

3.5 Other tabs

  • ICM: ICM (and FGS) calculator for the stacks, without solving anything.
  • Log: the engine's output. The first place to look if something is slow or fails.

Lab options (iterations, threads, update scheme, report interval, periodic NashConv, general re-raise size, maximum raises, bounty α, percentile river buckets, per-street buckets, pool boards, bunching) are not in the interface: the defaults are the right ones. They are still available on the command line (solve --help).

4. Choosing the settings

The defaults are a good choice in almost every case. This section explains when to change them.

4.1 Profile: push/fold or standard

Effective stackRecommended profile
≤ 10-12 bbPush/fold. It is what is actually played, and it converges in seconds.
12-20 bbStandard with all-in as an option (the all-in threshold adds it automatically). Compare with push/fold if in doubt.
> 20 bbStandard.

In push/fold, limped pots (SB complete) can be played postflop with the postflop option; without it they go straight to showdown.

4.2 Max active players

  • 3 (default) covers almost everything relevant: cold calls and squeezes.
  • 4 at short-handed tables with short stacks where 4-way all-ins are common (push/fold with 4-6 players). Much more expensive in standard trees.
  • 2 only for quick heads-up studies or when the tree does not fit.

4.3 Postflop precision

PrecisionUse
Normal (256 buckets)The standard. Recommended.
High (1024 buckets)Somewhat more accurate, more memory and time. Useful in deep PKO, where the value of seeing a flop matters more.

4.4 FGS (Future Game Simulation)

Plain ICM "freezes" the value of the chips at this hand. FGS simulates the following hands in push/fold: a short SB about to move to the button is not undervalued, and a player about to pay the big blind next hand is not overvalued.

  • Use it at final tables and SNGs with short stacks (below ~20 bb), where future position changes decisions.
  • auto applies the rules: 2-4 players → 3 hands; 5-7 → 2; otherwise → 1.
  • Blind level increase in FGS: if blinds go up soon, enter from which simulated hand and with which blinds (in bb of the current level), e.g. 2@0.75/1.5/0.2.
  • Only has an effect with ICM.

4.5 Quality (CI)

The solver does not ask how many iterations to run: it continues until the CI (convergence indicator) drops below the target of the chosen quality.

QualityCIMeaningUse
Quick20Still changing somewhatA quick idea of the spot.
Study10Stable: opens, flats and 3-bets settledNormal study. Recommended.
Reference3Mixes and marginal hands settledIn-depth review or sharing.

Approximate times to CI 10 with 16 threads:

SpotApproximate time
3-player push/fold, 10 bb< 10 s
Heads-up 25 bb with postflop~30 s
4-max 25 bb standard with postflop1.5-2 min
5-9 player push/fold ICM2 min – 1 h (depending on players and max active)
Deep 4-max with 4-way all-inshours
Deep 6-max PKO MTT45 min – 3 h

Reference (CI 3) usually takes several times as long as Study.

4.6 Prize models

  • Chip EV: no prizes. For cash-like play, early stages or as a baseline.
  • ICM: the table's remaining prizes. The last paid place is the last one you enter; players below it get 0.
  • Multi-table ICM: adds the stacks of the other tables. Essential near an MTT bubble: with only your table, ICM over- or underestimates the pressure.
  • PKO: progressive at 50 % by default (half of the bounty is paid out, the other half is added to yours). If the site uses a different split, change it (--ko 100% or the site's %); SKO pays the whole bounty.

5. Reading the results

5.1 CI and NashConv

  • CI measures how much the strategy is still changing. It is cheap and reported at every progress report.
  • NashConv (in the Log, at the end) measures how much an opponent exploiting you optimally could win, summed over players. It is the real measure of distance to equilibrium. Rough values: < 0.01 bb is very good in postflop spots, < 0.001 in push/fold.

A low CI with a high NashConv points to a badly designed tree (e.g. a node that is almost never reached and does not converge), not to a solver problem.

5.2 Mixed frequencies

A hand that comes out 50/50 between two actions is almost always indifferent: both actions are worth the same. Check EV vs best other: if the difference is cents or hundredths of a bb, either action is correct. Do not memorize exact mixes of indifferent hands; memorize the thresholds where the EV difference is large.

5.3 EV relative to folding

EVs in the grid and in exports are relative to folding at that same node (fold = 0). An EV of +$0.40 for a call means calling is worth $0.40 more than folding, not that you win $0.40 in the hand. If the node has no fold (e.g. the BB after limps), the EV is absolute.

5.4 Improbable nodes

Very rare lines (small open → shove → call → re-shove → the original caller decides again) converge poorly in any solver. Their ranges can look strange and it does not matter: they carry almost no weight in the total EV. When comparing solutions, always weight by the probability of reaching the node.

6. Study recipes

6.1 Reviewing a hand you played

  1. ＋ New hand → 📋 Import hand history → pick the hand.
  2. Add the remaining prizes (and other tables for an MTT).
  3. Profile according to the effective stack (§4.1). Preview the tree and prune unused sizes.
  4. Solve to CI < 10.
  5. Go to your decision node, find your hand and look at EV vs mejor otra (EV vs best alternative): that is what your play cost (or gained).

6.2 Sensitivity to a parameter

To find out whether a decision depends on a detail (an opponent's stack, a prize):

  1. Solve the base hand.
  2. Duplicate, change only that parameter and solve.
  3. Compare both grids at the same node.

6.3 Bubble and ICM pressure

Solve the same spot in Chip EV and in ICM (or multi-table ICM). The difference between the two calling ranges is the ICM pressure; between the shoving ranges, the extra fold equity it gives you.

6.4 Exploiting an opponent (node locking)

  1. Solve the equilibrium.
  2. At the opponent's node, use ✎ Paint lock with what you think they do (e.g. call only QQ+), or use --freq to lock only their frequency and let the solver choose the combos.
  3. Solve again: your strategy in the following nodes is the maximal response against that locked opponent.

Lock with care: exploiting a wrong read loses more than playing the equilibrium.

6.5 Refining a specific node

If an important node does not quite converge, 🎯 Refine this spot in the node panel (on the command line, --subtree "CO:R2 BTN:F" with --load) iterates only that subtree with the solution's incoming ranges, much faster than the whole tree.

7. Command line

solve.exe --help lists every option. Common recipes:

# Heads-up push/fold, chip EV
solve --stacks 10,10 --iters 500

# 6 players, ICM with other tables
solve --stacks 12,18,25,9,15,20 --payouts 50,30,20 --offtable 16x25,2x40 --iters 300

# Standard 4-max 25 bb to CI 10, with exports
solve --stacks 25,25,25,25 --profile standard --target-ci 10 --iters 3000 --export ranges.tsv --export-ev ev.tsv

# Automatic FGS at a final table
solve --stacks 8,25,12,30,6,19 --payouts 50,30,20 --fgs auto

# PKO with bounties and other tables
solve --stacks 15,30,22 --payouts 50,30,20 --bounties 10,10,10,10 --offtable 25

# From a hand history (bounties included if present)
solve --hh hand.txt --hand 3 --payouts 50,30,20 --html report.html

# ICM calculator without solving
solve icm --stacks 20,15,10 --payouts 50,30,20

# Save, continue and refine a subtree
solve --spot myspot.spot --iters 500 --save sol.bin
solve --spot myspot.spot --load sol.bin --iters 1500 --save sol.bin
solve --spot myspot.spot --load sol.bin --subtree "CO:R2 BTN:F" --iters 500

# Show the tree without solving (JSON)
solve preview --stacks 25,25,25,25 --profile standard

A spot file (--spot) has one option per line, key = value, and # for comments; it is the way to avoid very long command lines:

# 4-max 25 bb, ICM 50/30/20
stacks = 25,25,25,25
payouts = 50,30,20
profile = standard
max-in-pot = 3
target-ci = 10
iters = 3000

8. Performance

  • Close other calculations. Two calculations at once share the CPU and both take twice as long.
  • Small tree first. Tree size is the main lever: bet sizes, max active players and buckets, in that order.
  • Hybrid CPUs (Intel 12th gen or later). The engine takes itself out of Windows efficiency mode so it does not get stuck on the E-cores. If CPU usage still sits around 20-25 %, check in Task Manager that Efficiency mode is not enabled for solve.
  • Memory. Usage grows up to a ceiling and stays there; there are no leaks. If the preview estimates more RAM than you have, reduce buckets or max active players.
  • Cache. .cache/ saves minutes on every start. If you move the program folder, move the cache with it.

10. Glossary

  • Bucket: a group of hands that the postflop game treats as identical on a street.
  • Board bunching / card bunching: the effect of the cards held by players who folded on the other players' hands and on the board.
  • CFR+ / MCCFR: regret-minimization algorithms that converge to equilibrium; MCCFR samples boards instead of enumerating all of them.
  • CI: convergence indicator: how much the strategy is still changing (§4.5).
  • Closing flats: whoever closes the preflop action can always call.
  • FGS: simulation of future hands to value chips better than plain ICM.
  • Flats: calls without raising allowed per level.
  • Multi-table ICM: ICM that includes the stacks at the tournament's other tables.
  • NashConv: the sum of what each player would gain by exploiting the others; 0 at the exact equilibrium.
  • Node locking: fixing a player's strategy at a node to compute the best response.
  • OCHS: a way to group river hands by their strength against 8 groups of opponents.
  • PKO / SKO: progressive bounty (part of the bounty grows) / standard bounty (paid in full).
  • Pruning: removing an action from a specific node of the tree.
  • Raise over limp: a raise after one or more players limped in.
  • SPR: stack-to-pot ratio; with a low SPR the postflop game adds all-in as an option.
